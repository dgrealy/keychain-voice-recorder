I've wondered recently what my day would look like if money wasn't an object and I had nowhere to be. I like the idea of a day like Newton's or Darwin's, where they had a walk around their garden and crafted their day so that they didn't spend a huge amount of time physically working, but instead built their day around making the time they were working as high-quality as possible.

That would be really cool: doing maybe two or three 90-minute stints in the day and building the first six to eight hours of my day around those four and a half hours. I'd sprinkle in things like slow walks without aim, food, some sort of exercise like yoga, and meditation. I’d also have some gardening—all things that let me go into a default state where things are being processed in my subconscious. It would be relaxing and not highly stimulating, but not boring. 

In the evening, there would be some sort of organized training with other people on a specific time schedule. I would also need to manage food, relationships, and friends, and get things ready so that the next day looks as unimpinged and consistent as the first portion of the day I described. That means packing my bags, making food, and having things semi-clean.

I think it’s important to get as quickly as possible from bed to focused work. Having the feeling, whether it's valid or not, of having done a lot already in the day can affect my ability to work well. I want to make that transition as small as possible: maybe just a shower, getting my breakfast out of the fridge, and going.
